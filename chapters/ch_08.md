# Chapter 8 — Inside Job

## PAGE 71

[ART: Brooke driving her truck back into Crystal's Cove in the heavy, humid late-afternoon heat; the dashboard clock glows '4:45 PM'; Teepop sits in the passenger seat, looking down at the notes Brooke took in the basement vault]

The late-afternoon sun was a flat, brassy disc hanging over the cypress swamp, casting long, bruised shadows of Spanish moss across the gravel driveway of Crystal's Cove. The air inside my truck cabin was heavy and hot, even with the vents blowing at full blast. On the passenger seat between us, the sketch of the smudged courthouse signatures sat beside the original toxicology notes. 

"Albert Hebert," Teepop said, his voice dropping into that quiet, solemn register he used when he was reading a bad medical directive. "Miss Rena's late husband. He signed the search termination waiver, Brooke. He co-signed it with your stepfather Larry."

"Thirty years ago," I said, my hands holding the steering wheel tight, my leather duster draped over the back of the seat. "Larry was a deputy. Albert was the church search liaison. They signed the paper that stopped the sheriff's recovery team from searching the tupelo ridge. The exact ridge where Clara Hebert's body was supposed to have drowned."

"And the chemical in her lungs," Teepop muttered, "that freshwater herbicide, hexazinone. If they had searched the ridge back then, they would have found the poisoning site. They would have found the place where she was murdered. But Albert signed the form."

"Rena's family," I said, the words tasting like gravel. "It explains why she went so cold last night. Why she told me to drop the case. The cover-up that buried Clara didn't just belong to the parish suits, Tee. It belonged to the people inside our own circle."

I pulled the truck into our lot, the engine shutting off with a tired, shuddering gasp. 

"The trust," I said, looking out at the neat rows of mobile homes, "is cracking."

## PAGE 72

[ART: Brooke and Teepop standing on the porch of Lot 1; the twilight sky is a deep, bruised plum; Cricket stands alert at the top of the steps, looking toward the lit trailers of the Cove]

We stood on the Lot 1 porch, the sky turning a deep, bruised plum color that matched the shadows under my eyes. The neon string lights of the Cove were beginning to flicker on, cast-iron pink and electric turquoise bulbs glowing against the faded aluminum siding of the trailers. It looked beautiful, glamorous in that cheap, Southern way, but tonight it felt like a stage set.

"If McMurrary and the Moutons cleared the land thirty years ago," Teepop said, "they're consolidating it now. But to do that without a state audit, they need active, real-time information from inside the park. Someone is feeding them details about Bobby Boudreaux. Someone who knew about his binder. Someone who helped stage the trailer."

"We have to look inside, Tee," I said, my blue gloves tucked into my duster pocket, my boots planted firm. "The warning note on my truck was printed on professional office stock. But it was placed by someone who knew exactly where I park, who knew my routine. The threat came from Da Berry, but the hand that delivered it is right here."

Cricket stood alert at the top of the steps, her scruffy gray chin pointed toward the center of the Cove, her ears perked up at the sound of a distant lawnmower motor. 

"It's not a stranger, Tee," I whispered, watching Dale’s security golf cart rattle past the water tower in the distance, its yellow warning light spinning slow. "It's someone we wave to every morning."

## PAGE 73

[ART: Ricky Thibodaux standing on the Lot 1 porch, wearing safety goggles on his forehead and a faded defunct-startup tee; he is nervously gesturing toward a rugged industrial tablet showing a green lawnmower icon and a red path track]

"Brooke, Brooke, listen, I-I didn't mean to hack the parish council server, I swear!" 

Ricky Thibodaux was standing on our porch steps, his safety goggles pushed up onto his sweaty forehead, wearing a faded gray t-shirt with a cracked logo for a defunct online grocery startup. He was clutching a rugged, rubber-cased diagnostic tablet, his cargo shorts pockets stuffed with green-and-white circuit boards. 

"What did you do, Ricky?" I asked, my voice calm but firm as I walked to the edge of the porch. 

"It wasn't a hack, it was an open-source backup query!" he stammered, holding up the tablet. "I was running the diagnostic logs for Sheila. My autonomous lawnmower. She’s got a military-grade GPS tracker I salvaged from a marine surveying drone. I was checking her coordinates from the night of the crawfish boil, because... well, because Sheila kept wandering into Lot Five."

Teepop leaned over the screen, his lawyer eyes scanning the neat columns of digital data. "Ricky, this is a clean log path. Look at the timestamp."

"Exactly!" Ricky gasped, his eyes wide. "Sheila's aggression module was triggered at eleven-fifteen p.m. because she bumped into a solid, heavy obstacle in the dark behind Bobby's trailer. But look at the tracking path. The obstacle wasn't a tree. It was a vehicle. A vehicle that spent twenty-five minutes idling in the dark with its headlights off, right where Bobby's gnome collection was set up."

"What vehicle, Ricky?" I asked, my boots rooted to the floorboards.

"The thermal chassis signature," Ricky whispered, tapping the screen to zoom in on a green-and-black pixelated outline. "It was a custom-muffled electric utility cart. A golf cart, Brooke. With a double-wide tire assembly."

## PAGE 74

[ART: close shot of the tablet screen displaying the GPS path; a red line leads from Dale Scruggs' lot directly to the dark space behind Bobby Boudreaux's trailer, marked 'Obstacle Encountered at 11:15 PM']

My eyes fixed on the tablet screen, the green light casting long, high-contrast shadows across Ricky’s anxious face. 

The red line of the GPS path was a perfect, jagged trail. It started at Dale Scruggs' custom security lot, wound past the Cove dumpsters, and then crept quietly along the dark marsh edge before stopping dead behind Lot 5. 

*Obstacle Encountered: 11:15 PM.*
*Duration: 25 minutes.*

"Dale," I muttered, the word heavy in my chest. 

Dale Scruggs. Our self-appointed "Director of Perimeter Security." The man who wore a tactical vest over Hawaiian shirts, who had spent the morning after the disappearance stringing online-bought "perimeter" tape around the scene, making sure nobody got too close to the trailer door.

"It makes sense," Teepop said, his hand resting on my shoulder. "Dale has the keys to the maintenance shed. He has fourteen trail cameras. If anyone wanted to make sure Bobby vanished without a trace, Dale could have cleared the camera logs. He could have moved the truck across two spots to make it look like a sloppy midnight escape."

"I don't believe it," I said, my teeth clicking. "Dale is a nuisance, but he’s not a killer. He’s the man who stringed caution tape around Alix's tricycle because the tire pressure was 'non-compliant.' He’s Cove family."

"Cove family," Teepop said quietly, "can still have a price, Bug."

## PAGE 75

[ART: Brooke confronting Dale Scruggs beside his security golf cart; Dale is sweating heavily, his hand nervously adjusting the Oakleys on his forehead; a shiny silver Cinder Cove keytag hangs from the ignition switch]

I didn't wait for the sun to go down completely. I walked straight down the gravel path to Dale’s security lot, my duster billowing slightly in the warm bayou breeze. Dale was standing beside his lifted, custom-painted camouflage golf cart, polishing the fake chrome bumper with a yellow microfiber cloth.

"Dale," I said, my boots crunching on the gravel.

Dale jumped, his yellow cloth dropping to the dirt. He scrambled to retrieve it, his tactical vest clattering against the cart frame. He adjusted the Oakleys on his forehead, his red face instantly breaking into a tight, nervous grin.

"Brooke! Captain! Just... running a standard diagnostic sweep of the perimeter tires," he stammered, his voice higher than usual. "Lots of evening humidity. Non-compliant moisture levels on the gravel. You know how it is."

I didn't smile. I walked closer, my gloved hand reaching out to tap the ignition key of his cart. 

Hanging from the brass ring was a brand-new, polished silver keytag. It was stamped with the elegant, modern logo of *Cinder Cove Development.*

"Nice keytag, Dale," I said, my voice cold as ice. "I didn't know the parish security director was on the developer payroll."

Dale went pale, a thick bead of sweat running down the side of his cheek, disappearing into the collar of his faded palm-tree shirt. "It's... it's just a promotional item, Brooke! From the marketing booth at the parish fair! It doesn't mean nothing, I swear!"

"You were behind Bobby's trailer at eleven-fifteen on Tuesday night, Dale," I said, leaning closer until his Oakleys reflected my dark, dangerously focused eyes. "You spent twenty-five minutes in the dark. What did you do with him?"

## PAGE 76

[ART: Crystal Mouton sweeping into the shot, wearing bedazzled glasses on a chain and carrying her monogrammed clipboard; she is holding a large, glossy paper with list entries; Dale shrinks back against his camo golf cart]

"Now, Brooke Barnett, honey, bless your heart, what is all this high-volume neighborhood tension?"

Crystal Mouton entered the lot like a small, athletic hurricane. She was wearing her signature bedazzled glasses on a chain, her initials-in-rhinestones clipboard held high like a shield, her managerial athleisure bright under the twilight sky. Her smile was wide and artificial, but her eyes were tight and frantic as she looked between me and Dale.

"Crystal," I said, not shifting my gaze from Dale's sweating face. "Your security director was tracking Bobby's trailer in the dark on the night he vanished. And he’s carrying developer keys."

"Oh, Dale’s always so dramatic about his little patrol routines," Crystal laughed, her voice high and metallic as she walked between us, attempting to act as a buffer. "He was just clearing the park asset margins, sweetheart. We’re working on the landscaping plat. There’s really no need to bother Dale with... official business."

But as she gestured with her clipboard, the paper flipped slightly in the bayou breeze. 

My clinical eyes caught the sheet underneath her glossy HOA notices. It was a heavy, carbon-copy parish form labeled *'Easement Consolidation Ledger — Crystal's Cove.'* And at the very bottom, in Crystal's neat, looping purple ink, was a handwritten entry: 

*Lot 5 — Presumed Cleared. 11:30 PM.*

"Crystal," I said, my gloved hand reaching out like a hawk. "Show me that paper."

## PAGE 77

[ART: Brooke snatching the clipboard from Crystal's hand; Crystal's face is shocked and angry, her bedazzled glasses swinging; Brooke points her blue-gloved finger at the handwritten timestamp '11:30 PM']

I didn't ask. I simply reached out and snatched the monogrammed clipboard clean out of Crystal's manicured fingers. 

"Brooke! Sweetheart! That is private HOA documentation!" Crystal gasped, her face flushing red, her bedazzled glasses swinging wildly on their chain as she lunged to retrieve it. "You have no legal standing to confiscate Cove administration materials! I am the manager of this community!"

I stepped back, my boots planted firm in the gravel, my blue-gloved finger pointing straight at the carbon-copy entry at the bottom of the ledger.

"You told the sheriff you were at home in bed by ten-thirty on Tuesday night, Crystal," I said, my voice dropping into that quiet, dangerous register that always made the room go silent. "But your own handwritten maintenance log says you were clearing Lot Five at eleven-thirty p.m. The exact window when Bobby's TV was still on and his truck was parked crooked."

"I... I was just checking the utility boxes!" she stammered, her high-strung manager armor showing a massive, jagged crack. "The storm had knocked out the back meters! It was a routine safety check, Brooke!"

"With a Cinder Cove easement clearance form, Crystal?" I pressed, my voice relentless. "You were inside his trailer. You signed the clearance before Bobby’s truck had even cooled down."

"It's... it's a legal consolidation!" she purred, her voice dropping into a desperate, frantic pitch. "It’s for the good of the park, Brooke! We were going to get new paved roads!"

## PAGE 78

[ART: Crystal cracking under Brooke's interrogation; she holds her hands up defensively, her monogrammed clipboard flat on the golf cart hood; her eyes are wide and panicked under the twilight light]

"The paved roads were a lie, Crystal," I said, tossing the clipboard onto the camo hood of Dale’s golf cart. "You signed a contract with Bayou Ridge Holdings. A shell company registered in Delaware three days before Bobby filed his first complaint. They offered you a ten percent share of the Phase Three development. Ten percent of a luxury Cinder Cove retention pond, and the park manager's crown for life."

Crystal froze. Her hands went flat against the clipboard, her manicured acrylics digging into the plastic frame. She looked at me, the manager’s polish finally dropping away to reveal the raw, desperate survival-math underneath.

"You don't understand, Brooke," she whispered, her voice cracking, her eyes wide and panicked. "The park was going under. The parish was going to condemn the southern drainage lines anyway. Conrad... Conrad told me if we consolidated the lots quietly, we could save the front half of the Cove. Bobby Boudreaux was going to ruin everything! He was going to file that petition and trigger a state audit that would have closed the whole park down!"

"So you made him go away?"

"No!" Crystal gasped, her hands flying to her chest. "I swear to you, Brooke! I didn't hurt Bobby! I went to his trailer at eleven-thirty to offer him a cash buyout. But when I opened the screen door, the trailer was already empty. The TV was static. The bleach smell was so strong it made my eyes water. I... I saw his complaint binder on the counter, and I took the lot-line page because I was terrified of what would happen if the sheriff found it. But I didn't touch him!"

"And the bleach?" I asked, my clinical eyes fixed on her posture.

"It was already there," she whispered. "Someone had cleaned the floor before I arrived."

## PAGE 79

[ART: Dale Scruggs stepping forward, holding his tactical vest with both hands, his expression a mix of shame and desperate honesty; the camo golf cart sits in the background under the dark water tower]

"She’s telling the truth, Brooke," Dale said, stepping forward. He held his tactical vest with both hands, his Oakleys pushed back onto his forehead to reveal his red, ashamed eyes. "I... I was there, Captain. I'm sorry."

"Dale," I said, my boots shifting in the gravel. "Talk to me."

"Crystal offered me the custom lifted cart and a free security contract for Phase Three if I kept my mouth shut about the late-night visits," Dale muttered, not looking at me. "I took the deal. I’m a fool, Brooke. But at eleven-fifteen, I got nervous. I saw a black sedan creep past my camera loop without its headlights on. I drove my cart back to Lot Five to check on Bobby. But when I got behind the trailer, the sedan was already driving away toward the southern bayou road."

"Did you see the driver?"

"No," Dale said, shaking his head. "It was too dark. But the car... it had that silver medical plaque on the bumper. The same one Dr. McMurrary has. And when I went inside, Bobby's prize garden gnomes—the ones he had set up in a perfect row along the utility lot line—were messed up. One of them was completely gone."

"One gnome," I said, my mind tracking the coordinates Ricky’s drone had mapped. 

"Yeah," Dale said. "The blue one. The one Bobby called 'The Watchman.' Crystal didn't take it, and I didn't touch it. Whoever was in that black sedan took the gnome, Brooke. They took it like they were looking for something inside it."

The sedan. The medical plaque. Scott McMurrary's car.

The pieces were finally locking into a single, terrifying shape.

## PAGE 80

[ART: dramatic close shot on Brooke's face as she holds Bobby's unfiled complaint binder; her eyes are bright with absolute, clinical certainty as she points to a handwritten line in the notebook]

I walked back to Lot 1, my boots heavy on the gravel, the twilight having fully dissolved into the deep, dark Louisiana night. Teepop was waiting on the porch, my brass magnifying glass held over Bobby Boudreaux’s unfiled complaint binder.

"Did you find the leak?" Teepop asked.

"Crystal," I said, snapping off my blue gloves. "She was bribed with a ten percent cut of the development. She took the lot-line page, but she didn't take Bobby. Scott McMurrary’s black sedan was at the trailer at eleven-fifteen. And they took the blue garden gnome."

"The blue gnome?" Teepop said, his forehead furrowed. "Why would a Da Berry coroner steal a thirty-dollar plastic gnome?"

I flipped Bobby's binder open to the very first page, my finger pointing to a short, handwritten note Bobby had scribbled in the margin of his initial zoning complaint: 

*'If they try to clear the utility line, the watchman stands where the light don't shine.'*

"The watchman," I whispered, the fire in my chest turning cold and sharp. "Bobby didn't just document the land grab, Tee. He had evidence. Soil samples from the tupelo ridge. The freshwater herbicide coordinates. He knew they were hunting for his cache, so he hid it inside the blue gnome. But he didn't leave the gnome in his yard."

"Where is it, Brooke?"

I looked up at the dark, rusted outline of the old Crystal's Cove water tower rising against the stars.

"The old pump house," I said. "The only spot in the Cove that sits in total shade twenty-four hours a day. The watchman is buried under the concrete foundation of the pump house, Tee. And we're going to dig it up."
