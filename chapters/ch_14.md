# Chapter 14 — Treasure Hunt & Lineage

## PAGE 131

[ART: wide establishing shot of Crystal Mouton's double-wide luxury residence at twilight; the yard is decorated with pristine white gravel, glowing pink neon flamingos, and a faux-antique sign reading "Crystal's Retreat"; inside the cab of Brooke's faded truck, the found family is crowded in a tight circle under the warm dashboard glow]

The twilight had turned the Louisiana sky the color of a bruised plum, the heavy summer humidity hanging thick and sweet over the Tupelo swamp. Crystal Mouton’s double-wide luxury residence sat at the far end of the Cove like a plastic palace, its white vinyl siding pristine under the soft, pink glow of her custom neon lawn flamingos. 

Inside the cab of my truck, the air was warm and smelled of Sugar’s banana pudding, damp leather, and the faint, coppery tang of the marsh clay on our boots. 

I looked around the circle. Teepop sat in the middle, his broad shoulders pressed against the window, his laptop screen reflecting a green grid of the Cove's coordinates in his glasses. Next to him, Miss Rena sat regal and silent, her dark hand resting steady on Cricket's scruffy back. In the back jump seat, Sugar McCoy was adjusted in her leopard housecoat, Dale Scruggs was adjusting his tactical vest over a loud yellow Hawaiian shirt, and Ricky Thibodaux was checking the wiring on his backyard explorer exploration tool.

For thirty years, I’d carried the weight of Eret alone, thinking every door I opened had to be chiseled by my own knuckles. I thought that to protect the powerless, I had to keep everyone else out of the dark. 

But looking at them now—this messy, loud, banana-pudding-eating, tactical-golf-cart-driving family—the steel in my spine didn't feel cold anymore. It felt warm. 

"Okay, Bug," Teepop said, his voice dropping into that deep, reassuring tone that always anchored my chest. "We’ve got ten minutes before Crystal’s parish zoning call ends. Let's make it count."

## PAGE 132

[ART: Sugar McCoy and Tammy Beaumont standing on Crystal's front porch; Sugar, in her vibrant leopard housecoat, is holding a steaming foil-wrapped baking dish in one hand and shaking her fist at the door, while Tammy, in giant gold hoops and leopard print, is waving her long acrylic nails in a dramatic, loud complaint]

"Crystal Lee Mouton!" 

Sugar McCoy’s voice cut through the heavy swamp air like a brass horn, her fist pounding on Crystal’s double-pane glass front door with enough force to shake the neon flamingos. She was holding a massive, steaming foil-wrapped dish of peach cobbler in her left hand, using it like a shield as she leaned into the screen.

"I know you in there listin' to your motivational parish cassettes, Crystal!" Sugar shouted, her leopard housecoat fluttering in the hot breeze. "We got a crisis on Lot Two, and as the self-appointed queen of this HOA, you are obligated to notice! The drainage easement is backin' up into my hydrangeas, and it smells like a Da Berry municipal dump! And don't you get me started on the unauthorized lawnmowers!"

Behind her, Tammy Beaumont stood in her giant gold hoops, waving her package-opening acrylic nails with dramatic flair. 

"It's a zoning disaster, Crystal!" Tammy cried, her voice carrying a heavy, dry-wine vibration across the gravel. "My backyard fermentation license is being compromised by these fumes! We need an immediate, high-level administrative consultation! Open this door before I write a letter to the parish board myself!"

Inside the house, the shadow of a velvet scrunchie moved behind the frosted glass. The lock clicked, the door swung open, and Crystal Mouton stepped out, monogrammed clipboard held high, completely drawn into the trap.

## PAGE 133

[ART: Dale Scruggs sitting alert in his customized tactical golf cart under the live oaks, his Oakleys resting on his forehead; Ricky Thibodaux is crouched by the side door of Crystal's house, pointing his modified explorations box at the smart lock; Sheila the autonomous mower is wedged in the hydrangeas nearby]

"Perimeter clear, Bug," Dale Scruggs muttered into his tactical earpiece, his Oakley sunglasses resting steady on his forehead. He sat in the driver’s seat of his custom golf cart, his yellow Hawaiian shirt bright against the dark live oak shadows, his hand resting on the cart's upgraded heavy-duty steering wheel. "Sugar’s got her locked in a peach cobbler debate. You’ve got five minutes before she checks the side monitors."

"Copy that, Dale," I whispered, slipping through the shadow of the hibiscus bushes toward the utility door. 

Ricky Thibodaux ran beside me, his safety goggles resting on his forehead, his defunct-startup t-shirt damp with sweat. He knelt by the digital keypad of Crystal's side entrance, pointing his customized exploration exploration box—a messy nest of wires and green circuit boards—at the glowing lock. 

"The firmware on these Cinder Cove smart-locks is standard parish grade, Brooke," Ricky whispered, his fingers moving fast. "I'm just spoofing the admin signal from the main pump house loop."

Below the porch, Sheila, the autonomous Husqvarna lawnmower, rolled slowly into the hydrangeas with a soft, mechanical *whir.* Her grabber arm twitched, her customized aggression module letting out a tiny beep as she wedged herself into the stalks, creating the perfect cover of lawn-noise.

*Click.* 

The green light on the keypad flashed, and the side door swung silent into the dark.

## PAGE 134

[ART: Brooke stepping inside Crystal's private office; the room is decorated in ultra-styled pink leather, with gold-framed corporate slogans reading 'Standards don't lower themselves' on the wall; a glass-top desk sits under a neon-pink lamp, smelling of vanilla and bleach]

I stepped into Crystal Mouton's private office, and my clinical nurse's nose immediately picked up the scent: heavy vanilla aerosol spray, underneath a sharp, clean layer of high-strength chlorine bleach. 

Someone had been cleaning. And in my experience, people only clean that fast when they're ashamed of what they've left behind.

The room was the ultimate expression of Crystal's "boss-babe" lifestyle brand. Two plush, rose-pink leather armchairs sat opposite a massive glass-top desk, their gold-plated legs glittering under a neon-pink desk lamp. On the wood-paneled wall behind the desk, a series of gold-framed corporate slogans were arranged in a perfect grid: 

*STANDARDS DON'T LOWER THEMSELVES.*
*SUCCESS IS 90% OPTICS, 10% COMPLIANCE.*

On the desk sat Crystal's backup monogrammed clipboard, a neat stack of laminated lot plat maps, and a heavy brass pen holder shaped like a velvet scrunchie. 

I pulled my thin nitrile gloves from my duster pocket, the blue latex snapping tight against my wrists. 

"Keep watch at the seam, Ricky," I whispered, my brown eyes scanning the desk. "The document we need isn't going to be on the glass. Crystal’s too careful for that."

## PAGE 135

[ART: close shot on Brooke's gloved hands opening the bottom drawer of Crystal's white filing cabinet; her fingers are tracing a folder marked 'Cinder Cove Developers — Confidential'; a gold-plated key lies in the drawer's corner]

My hyper-awareness settled into that quiet, familiar groove as I knelt before the white filing cabinet in the corner. 

I didn't rush. I didn't pull folders at random. I looked at the dust lines on the cabinet pulls first. The top drawer was clean—daily maintenance ledgers, HOA dues, complaints about Dale’s cart. The bottom drawer, however, had a faint, double-edged smudge near the keyhole, the gray metal showing the tiny, circular scratches of a key turned in a hurry.

I slid the bottom drawer open, the suspension tracks letting out a soft, metallic slide. 

My fingers traced the green hanging folders, reading the color-coded tabs: *Zoning Amendments, Parish Holds, Lot 5 Easement.* 

And then, tucked behind a thick binder of parish consolidation drafts, my hand brushed a heavy, dark-blue folder labeled: *Cinder Cove Developers — Confidential.*

Resting in the very back of the folder, partially hidden under a stack of parish notary seals, was a heavy, gold-plated key with a miniature blue plastic tag reading: *Cinder Cove Safe-Deposit #412.*

"Brooke," Ricky whispered from the doorway, his voice trembling slightly. "Dale says Crystal’s looking at her watch. Sugar’s running out of cobbler facts."

"I've got the key, Ricky," I said, my fingers sliding a yellowed envelope out from the same file. "But there's something else in here."

## PAGE 136

[ART: Brooke standing under the neon-pink lamp, her eyes wide as she reads a handwritten letter on yellowed paper; the envelope is postmarked 'Eret, 1989'; the fine-line bee tattoo is visible on her forearm]

I pulled the yellowed letter from the envelope, the paper dry and brittle under my gloved fingers. The return address in the corner was written in a beautiful, flowing script that I would recognize anywhere: *Kat Barnett, Lot 12, Eret, LA.*

Postmarked May 12, 1989. 

My breath caught in my throat, the pink neon light casting a soft, rose-colored shadow over the bee tattoo on my forearm. 

*Dear Crystal,* the letter began, the ink fading but clear. *I am signing the assistant block on the coroner registry like Larry said. I know what he did in the vault, and I know he has the Da Berry records. But you have to promise me, Crystal. You have to keep the easement open for Rena and Albert. They've put their whole lives into that ridge. If you consolidate the tupelo line, they lose their home. I am carrying this shame so my Brooke doesn't have to grow up under Larry's shadow. Let the easement stand, or the state council gets the photos Clara took.*

The paper trembled in my hand. 

For thirty years, I’d believed my mother Kat was just a silent victim—worn down by fear, signing McMurrary’s lies because she had no choice. But she wasn't just surviving. She was negotiating. She was trading her own signature to build a wall of protection around Miss Rena, around the Cove, and around me. 

She was doing the forensic math to keep us safe.

"Kat," I whispered, the recovery warmth spreading through my chest, dissolving the cold Eret ghost that had haunted my steps for decades. "You didn't abandon the truth. You just hid it where I could find it."

## PAGE 137

[ART: close shot on the document in Brooke's hand; it is a carbon-copy chemical ledger sheet co-signed by McMurrary and Conrad Mouton Sr., showing a bulk order of forty drums of hexazinone herbicide, dated May 10, 1989]

Tucked directly behind my mother's letter was the final, missing piece of the Tupelo Ridge puzzle. 

It was a physical carbon-copy ledger sheet, the pink duplicate paper faded but perfectly readable under my magnifying glass. The header was printed with the bold, industrial logo of the *Acadian Chemical Corporation, Lafayette District.*

Dated May 10, 1989—two days before Clara Hebert’s body was recovered from the canal.

*CUSTOMER: Conrad Mouton Sr., Cinder Cove Developers.*
*MATERIAL: 40 x 55-Gallon Drums Hexazinone Freshwater Herbicide (Concentrated).*
*SHIPPING COORDINATES: Section 12 Tupelo Ridge Easement.*

At the bottom of the page, in dark, carbon-pressed ink, were two signatures: 

*Conrad Mouton Sr. (Purchaser)*
*Dr. Scott McMurrary (Parish Coroner, Clinical Approval)*

The purchase receipts. The physical, undeniable link proving the coroner and the developer didn't just cover up Clara’s death—they bought the exact chemical that poisoned the water, then staged her runaway drowning to hide the evidence.

"This is it, Ricky," I said, my voice steady as steel. "This is the chain of custody. The record is complete."

## PAGE 138

[ART: Brooke standing in the office as the ceiling crown molding flashes with bright, pulsing red LED alarm lights; the digital keypad by the door is glowing red, and Brooke is pressing her earpiece as she hears footsteps in the hall]

*BEEP. BEEP. BEEP.*

A sharp, high-pitched electronic chime cut through the silence of the office, and the pink neon lamp died, replaced instantly by a series of pulsing red LED alarm lights along the white crown molding. 

The lock on the utility door let out a heavy, automated *CLACK*, the green light on the keypad turning a solid, angry red.

"Bug! Bug, get out of there!" Teepop’s voice crackled urgent through my earpiece, the sound of static loud against my ear. "The main loop just went down! Crystal’s security hub picked up the override from the pump house! She’s running back across the lawn right now!"

"The side door is locked, Tee," I said, my voice dropping into that quiet, clinical register. "Keypad's dead-locked."

In the hallway outside the office, the sharp, rapid click of designer wedges sounded loud on the hardwood floors, accompanied by the rustle of monogrammed athleisure. 

"Ricky, slip into the closet," I whispered, sliding the ledger carbon and my mother’s letter deep into my duster’s inner pocket. "I’ll handle the queen."

The brass door handle turned, and the office door swung open.

## PAGE 139

[ART: Crystal Mouton standing in the doorway, wearing her designer monogrammed athleisure and bedazzled glasses; she is holding her signature clipboard like a shield, her face twisted in fury under the pulsing red alarm lights]

Crystal Mouton stood in the doorway, her velvet scrunchie slightly crooked, her bedazzled glasses glinting red under the pulsing alarm lights. She was wearing her pristine, white-and-rose designer athleisure, but her polished, managerial mask was completely gone, replaced by a raw, twisting fury.

She held her monogrammed clipboard tight against her chest like a shield.

"Brooke Barnett," she hissed, her voice vibrating with a sharp, desperate malice. "I knew you were a nuisance, sha. But breaking and entering? In my own home? The sheriff’s patrol is going to have you in a Da Berry cell before the midnight vote even closes!"

"The sheriff's loop is dead, Crystal," I said, standing tall under the red lights, my boots planted firm. "McMurrary is zip-tied in the swamp, Larry’s record is in the courthouse, and your Cayman wire transfers are already on Teepop’s nonprofit cloud."

"You don't understand progress, Brooke!" Crystal cried, her clipboard shaking in her manicured fingers. "This park was a ramshackle swamp before my family built the Cove! The lot consolidation is the only thing that keeps this parish from falling off the state map! So what if we cleared a little timber ridge thirty years ago? It was empty land!"

"Clara Hebert wasn't empty land, Crystal," I said, my voice cold as ice.

## PAGE 140

[ART: dramatic close shot on Brooke standing firm, holding the carbon-copy ledger sheet in her gloved hand; outside the window, Teepop's drone is hovering, its camera lens glowing red and active; Crystal's face shows the first crack of absolute defeat]

"My mother signed your cover-up because Larry stood over her with a glass," I said, stepping forward until the blue latex of my glove caught the red light. "But she made you promise to keep the easement open. She documented your crime to protect Miss Rena. And you violated that promise the second Cinder Cove offered you ten percent of the consolidation pipeline."

I held up the Acadian Chemical duplicate sheet, the pink carbon paper sharp against the red glow. 

"The hexazinone receipts," I said. "Signed by your father and McMurrary. The exact chemical that killed Clara Hebert."

Outside the window, a soft, high-pitched hum vibrated through the glass. Teepop’s drone hovered three feet from the screen, its camera lens glowing red and active, the live feed recording every word, every signature, and every confession.

Crystal looked at the drone, then at the pink carbon sheet, and finally into my eyes. 

Her monogrammed clipboard slipped from her fingers, clattering loud against the rose-pink leather chair before hitting the floor boards. Her shoulders slumped, her bedazzled glasses sliding crooked down her nose as the first crack of absolute defeat broke her face.

"The board hearing is in twelve hours, Crystal," I said, turning toward the door as Ricky bypassed the lock from inside. "And the Cove is going to be there to correct the record."
