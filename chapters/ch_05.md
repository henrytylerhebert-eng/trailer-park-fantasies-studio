# Chapter 5 — Confrontation

## PAGE 41

[ART: Brooke entering the humid, wood-paneled Da Berry Parish Zoning Board Office; Conrad Mouton sits behind a massive oak desk, wearing a polished gray suit and gold rings, looking up with a cool, patronizing expression]

The Da Berry Parish Zoning Board Office was a monument to the slow, damp passage of Southern bureaucracy. It smelled of old floor wax, air-conditioning mold, and fifty years of carbon-copy paper. The walls were lined with faded parcel maps of the Acadiana marshes, their margins curling from the humidity that seeped through the single-pane windows. Behind a massive, water-stained oak desk sat Conrad Mouton, Crystal’s ex-husband. He was wearing a polished gray suit that didn’t fit the local temperature, and his gold signet ring caught the fluorescent light as he tapped a fountain pen against a stack of folders.

"Brooke," he said, his voice flat and patronizing, like a bank teller explaining a service fee. "It’s been a long time since you were assisting Scott in the coroner's vault. What brings the Bayou Bloodhound out of Crystal's Cove?"

"A missing man, Conrad," I said, walking to his desk without waiting for an invitation. "And an unfiled complaint form that has your name circled in red ink."

He didn't flinch. He just set his pen down with a soft, slow click and leaned back in his leather chair, his face smooth and empty of anything resembling surprise. 

"Bobby Boudreaux was a chronic filer, Brooke," he said smoothly. "The man was a nuisance."

## PAGE 42

[ART: close shot on Conrad's desk as Brooke slaps Bobby's unfiled complaint form onto the oak surface; Conrad adjusts his collar, a thin bead of sweat visible on his jawline]

"He was a nuisance who caught you stealing the utility easement, Conrad," I said, slapping the unfiled parish complaint form onto the center of his oak desk.

Conrad looked down at the paper, his eyes scanning Bobby's thick, angry red ballpoint handwriting. His signet ring tapped once against the wood, and I watched his collar—just a slight, tight adjustment of the silk tie, a thin bead of sweat beginning to sheen along the corner of his jaw.

"This is an unfiled draft, Brooke," he said, his voice dropping into that cold, institutional rhythm I’d heard in a hundred county meetings. "It has no legal standing. Mr. Boudreaux’s concerns about the back acreage were evaluated. The zoning board handled the matter internally."

"Handled it?" I half-smirked, my boots planted firm. "By handing Crystal the key to his trailer? By signing a forged title transfer at midnight from Scott's office computer?"

"We resolved the boundary discrepancies, Brooke," he said, his voice tightening. "There is no need to make a formal report on a matter that has already been cleared by parish council decree. It’s an internal administration detail."

## PAGE 43

[ART: Conrad gesturing dismissively toward a stack of thick green files on his desk, his expression hard and bureaucratic under the fluorescent light]

"There is no file, Brooke, because there is no case," Conrad said, gesturing toward the thick stack of green folders on his desk. "Mr. Boudreaux was a debtor who abandoned his property. The Acadian Development title transfer was executed under Section Twelve. It’s perfectly legal."

"Section Twelve," I said, "is for estate clearance when a resident dies without heirs, Conrad. Bobby Boudreaux was alive on Tuesday night."

"The parish," he said, his voice hardening into a wall of absolute bureaucratic indifference, "has deemed the parcel abandoned. The filing is closed. If you want to argue the lot lines, you can file a petition with the district court in six months. Until then, there is no case, sha. And no need to make a report."

*No need to make a report.*

The words sat in the humid air of the office, cold and heavy as iron. They were the exact words the parish used when they wanted to stop looking. The words they used when they wanted to tell the powerless that their facts were just noise, and that the record had already decided what the truth was going to be.

## PAGE 44

[ART: dramatic close shot on Brooke's face; her eyes are dark and dangerously focused as she remembers her Eret backstory, the kitchen light casting long shadows on her jawline]

The words lit a fire in my chest. 

It wasn't a new fire. It was the same heat I’d carried since I was a twelve-year-old girl in Eret, sitting in a dark kitchen while my mother Kat wiped her split lip and the local deputy—Larry’s friend, Larry’s shift partner—stood by the door with his hands tucked into his gun belt. 

*“It’s a family matter, Kat,”* the deputy had said, his voice flat and dismissive as he let Larry sleep off the whiskey. *“No need to make a report. We handled it internally. Let’s keep it between us, sha.”*

That was the day I learned that protected men use institutional language to build their walls. They write the files so they can write the ending, and they dismiss the screams as gossip because gossip doesn't have a carbon copy. 

I looked at Conrad Mouton, at his polished gray suit and his signet ring, and I didn't see a zoning official. I saw Larry's badge. I saw every man who ever believed that a desk and a title gave him the right to make the truth disappear.

"Conrad," I said, my voice dropping into a quiet, dangerous register. "You're using the wrong language with me."

## PAGE 45

[ART: Brooke leaning over the oak desk, her gloved hand flat on the plat map, her face inches from Conrad's as he shrinks back slightly in his leather chair]

"I know the pattern, Conrad," I said, leaning over his massive oak desk until my face was inches from his. "You call the powerless 'difficult' so nobody listens. You call a staged trailer 'abandonment' so nobody looks. And you call a forged title transfer 'procedural clearance' so nobody writes it down."

Conrad shrank back slightly in his leather chair, his hands flat on the green folders. "Brooke, you're out of your lane. You're an ex-nurse who lives in a mobile home park. You don't have a badge."

"I don't need a badge to read a wound, Conrad," I said, my voice cold as steel. "My mother called the police, and they handled it internally. She slept in a car because they didn't make a report. I've spent my whole life seeing the things you pretend aren't there."

I tapped the plat map with my blue-gloved finger. "The coroner signed the survey, Conrad. Scott McMurrary. The man who taught me how to read the dead. You think I don't know his hand? You think I don't know how he hides a cut behind a clean margins?"

## PAGE 46

[ART: Conrad sweating, his signet ring trembling as he adjusts his glasses; the fluorescent office light casts harsh, high-contrast shadows on his face]

"Scott... Scott signed the plat because it was a parish consolidation, Brooke," Conrad muttered, his bureaucratic armor finally showing a hair-line crack. He adjusted his glasses, his signet ring trembling slightly against the folders. "He was clearing the utility easement. It was an administrative sign-off. Nothing more."

"An administrative sign-off on a case search sector, Conrad?" I pressed, my voice relentless. "Teepop found the tag. Registry eighty-nine-oh-four-twelve. The search sector for the girl who vanished thirty years ago."

Conrad went pale, the color draining out of his face until he looked like one of Scott's cold cases. "That... that was an old file, Brooke. A runaway. It has nothing to do with Acadian Development."

"Then why are you mapping the utility lot line directly over the carved cross in the tupelo?" I asked, my flashlight still in my coat pocket, my hand flat on his desk. "Why are you trying to dredge the bayou ridge?"

"We aren't dredging," he whispered, looking toward the door. "We're consolidating. That's all."

## PAGE 47

[ART: Conrad leaning forward, his voice low and defensive, while Brooke stands over him, capturing his words like evidence]

"It was Bobby," Conrad suddenly blurted, his voice low and defensive as he leaned forward. "Bobby was the one who wouldn't drop the zoning complaint. He filed a formal petition three weeks ago, Brooke. He claimed the survey markers were encroaching on the state water easement."

"And what did you do with the petition, Conrad?"

"I... I filed it," he muttered. "I put it in the secure cabinet in the back vault. Before he skipped town. I acted on it, Brooke. I acted on it quietly, to avoid a parish audit. We were going to adjust the markers."

"You acted on it," I said, my teeth clicking. "By taking his key and clearing his files."

"No!" Conrad gasped. "I didn't touch Bobby. I swear to you. Crystal... Crystal was the one who was coordinating the Cinder Cove developer. She said Bobby was handled. She said he'd agreed to the quitclaim."

"Crystal's ex-husband," I said, "and her HOA empire. You both had a reason to make him go away."

## PAGE 48

[ART: Crystal Mouton entering the office abruptly, wearing bedazzled glasses on a chain and carrying a monogrammed clipboard; she is smiling too wide, trying to diffuse the high tension]

The office door swung open abruptly, and Crystal Mouton entered like weather. 

She was wearing her bedazzled glasses on a chain and carrying her initials-in-rhinestones clipboard, her manager Athleisure bright under the dingy office lights. Her smile was charged all the way up, but her eyes were tight and frantic as she looked between me and her ex-husband.

"Conrad, honey, bless you, are we having a little parish reunion?" she purred, walking to the desk and attempting to act as a buffer. "Brooke, sweetheart, I told you the zoning board had already cleared the Cinder Cove optics. There’s really no need to bother Conrad with park business. We’re working on the landscaping plat."

"Crystal," I said, not shifting my gaze from Conrad. "Your ex-husband just told me Bobby's zoning petition was acted on quietly before he vanished."

"Oh, Conrad’s always so dramatic about paperwork," she laughed, her voice high and artificial. "Bobby was just a difficult man, Brooke. We resolved the boundary discrepancies. Let's let the neighborhood go back to its coffee, honey."

## PAGE 49

[ART: Brooke walking out of the heavy, humid zoning board building into the blinding Louisiana golden-hour sun; she is adjusting her duster, her face set in a calculating expression]

I walked out of the Da Berry Parish Zoning Board Office, leaving the screen door to slap shut behind me. The hot, humid air of the Louisiana golden hour hit my face like a wet towel, the sun still blinding as it sat low over the Da Berry parish courthouse.

I adjusted the collar of my duster, my boots crunching on the gravel of the parking lot.

Conrad Mouton was a classic middle-man. He was polished, bureaucratic, and easily rattled. He was too clean to be the ultimate mastermind behind a land-grab that utilized forged digital signatures and coroner's computers at midnight. He’d sweated the second I mentioned Scott McMurrary's name.

He was the gateway, yes. But he wasn't the boss.

The real target was still in the shadows—the man who had the authority to use Section Twelve, the man who had signed the plat with clinical precision, and the man who had the black sedan with the medical plaque on the bumper.

Dr. Scott McMurrary was the master of Da Berry's records.

## PAGE 50

[ART: close shot on the windshield of Brooke's truck; a typed, anonymous note is tucked tightly under the black wiper blade; the background is the misty parish parking lot in the twilight]

I walked to my truck, my hand reaching into my pocket for the keys. But as I approached the driver's side door, I went very still.

Tucked tightly under the black rubber of the windshield wiper blade was a folded, white piece of paper. It hadn't been there when I parked.

I snapped on a blue glove and pulled the paper out from under the wiper. I unfolded it slow.

Inside, printed in a cold, clean, computerized font, was a short, anonymous warning.

*Stop looking in the mud, Bloodhound.*
*Some bodies are buried for a reason.*
*Drop the case.*
*Or the next record Brooke Barnett corrects will be her own.*

I looked around the misty, twilight-shadowed parking lot. The cypress trees at the edge of the lot were dark, their Spanish moss swaying in the humid breeze like a row of silent witnesses.

The warning was clear. The case was now active danger.

Cricket growled from the passenger seat.

She's usually right.
