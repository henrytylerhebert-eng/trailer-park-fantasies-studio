# Chapter 10 — Double-Cross

## PAGE 91

[ART: Brooke and Teepop in the warm, lit kitchen of Lot 1; the chiseled blue garden gnome sits on the counter beside a rugged, glowing laptop screen; Teepop's fingers are hovering over the keyboard as he connects the watertight black flash drive]

The hum of the Lot 1 kitchen fridge felt loud in the midnight quiet, a steady, domestic vibration that did nothing to calm the wild, electric heat in my veins. The blue garden gnome sat on the Formica counter, its smiling plastic face smeared with dark bayou clay and chiseled concrete dust. Beside it, the watertight plumbing seal lay flat, smelling of grease and trapped dry air. 

Teepop booted up his secured, startup-encrypted laptop. The screen cast a cool, pale blue light over his glasses, his large shoulders hunched forward as he carefully inserted the rugged black USB flash drive into the side port.

"It’s a military-grade partition," Teepop said, his fingers beginning to fly across the keyboard, tapping out a series of legal decrypt algorithms. "Bobby didn't just drag-and-drop these files, Bug. He had an auto-backup script configured to mirror his parish complaint binder. It’s a clean mirror. The data is structured in three separate blocks: land deeds, corporate bank routing logs, and..."

"...and the original coroner files," I finished, my blue nitrile gloves still tight on my hands, my boots planted firm on the linoleum. "Let's read what's behind the wall, Tee."

Cricket sat at my boots, her chin resting on her front paws, her ears perked up at the soft, rhythmic clicking of the keyboard. 

"Here we go," Teepop whispered. "The partition is opening."

## PAGE 92

[ART: close shot on the laptop screen showing three digital directory folders labeled 'LOT_CONSOLIDATION_DEEDS', 'BAYOU_RIDGE_TRANSACTIONS', and 'REGISTRY_89-0412_RAW'; a green progress bar reads 'DECRYPTION COMPLETE']

The screen flashed a bright green, the terminal emulator console running a fast sweep of code lines before resolving into a clean, modern folder directory. 

*DECRYPTION COMPLETE.*
*AUTHORIZED ACCESS: SYSTEM ROOT.*

Three digital directory folders sat in the center of the desktop, their labels stark and clean under the glowing screen:

1. `LOT_CONSOLIDATION_DEEDS`
2. `BAYOU_RIDGE_TRANSACTIONS`
3. `REGISTRY_89-0412_RAW`

"Bobby was thorough," Teepop said, zooming in on the transaction folder. "Look at the transaction logs, Brooke. These aren't just corporate bank routing clearing numbers. These are wire transfers from a Delaware trust directly to Conrad Mouton’s private offshore account in Grand Cayman. Every single transfer matches the date of a parish zoning board consolidation decree."

"A pay-off pipeline," I said, my magnifying glass held over the screen. "Every time Conrad signed a Section Twelve estate clearance, Conrad's account got a twenty-thousand-dollar boost."

"But look who's financing the Delaware trust," Teepop said, his finger tapping the screen. "It’s not Cinder Cove Development. The money is coming from a private medical holding company called 'Lafayette Aesthetic Trust.'"

The clinical name sent a cold shiver down my spine.

"The doctor," I muttered.

## PAGE 93

[ART: Teepop pointing at a scanned, digital email printout on the screen; the email displays bold headers from 'smcmurrary@da-berry-coroner.org' to 'conrad.mouton@da-berry-parish.gov']

"It’s a double-cross inside the development itself," Teepop said, opening a sub-folder containing scanned, private emails. "Crystal Mouton and her developer suits thought they were the ones running the Cinder Cove expansion. They thought they were building a luxury mobile home community and a tennis club. But look at this thread, Brooke."

My eyes scanned the digital text, the headers bold and clear:

*FROM: smcmurrary@da-berry-coroner.org*
*TO: conrad.mouton@da-berry-parish.gov*
*SUBJECT: Easement consolidation - Lot 5.*

*'Conrad: The Cinder Cove developers are focused on the retention pond layout. Let them have the surface plat. But the dredging coordinates must be executed exactly along the tupelo ridge coordinates mapped in Registry 89-0412. The state environmental safety clearance must be signed off under Section Twelve before the Monday vote. If Bobby Boudreaux files his petition, the state auditor will scan the soil coordinates, and the hexazinone concentrations will trigger a federal toxic site designation. The historical record must remain buried under the foundation. Handle Boudreaux immediately. — S.M.'*

"McMurrary isn't just a gatekeeper," I whispered, the fire in my chest turning cold and sharp. "He’s the silent partner financing the entire Phase Three. He’s using Cinder Cove's retention pond as a legal mask to pour concrete over his own thirty-year-old crime."

"He double-crossed his own ex-wife," Teepop said. "He let Crystal take the risk from the inside, while he controlled the records from the top."

## PAGE 94

[ART: Brooke and Teepop listening to the laptop speakers as a green audio waveform plays; Brooke's face is a mix of deep relief and intense focus]

"There's an audio file, Brooke," Teepop said, clicking on a green digital waveform icon at the bottom of the directory: `memo_boudreaux_05-26.wav`. 

He tapped the play button, and the kitchen was instantly filled with the scratchy, high-pitched, inflected Cajun voice of Bobby Boudreaux. It was scratchy, recorded on a cheap phone microphone, but his loud, combative spirit was unmistakable.

*"Brooke... Bug... if you're playing this, sha, it means they finally came for my blue watchman."*

I went very still, my hand gripping Teepop's arm as a rush of deep, profound relief flooded my chest. 

*"I had to go to ground, Brooke. Tuesday night, after the crawfish boil, I was sitting in my trailer when I saw McMurrary's black sedan creep down the canal road with its headlights off. I knew Conrad had leaked my petition. I grabbed my complaint binder, stuffed the flash drive and my primary soil sample inside the blue watchman, and buried it under the pump house concrete. I’ve been hiding out in the old cypress camp ridge near Crowflies, Bug. I'm alive, sha. I'm staying in the mud until you correct the record. Don't let them pour the concrete. Don't let the doctor win."*

The voice memo clicked off into static.

"He's alive," Teepop whispered, his face breaking into a huge, warm smile. "Bobby’s alive, Brooke. He went to ground when he realized his measurements were right."

"He went to ground," I said, my boots planted firm. "But he left us the map."

## PAGE 95

[ART: Brooke standing tall, her face resolved as she checks the digital logs; Teepop leans back in his chair, smiling with relief; the shadow of suspicion is completely cleared from their Cove neighbors]

The confirmation was a clean sweep. 

The audio memo and the bank records completely cleared the red herrings that had shadowed the Cove since the incitement morning. Dale Scruggs wasn't a traitor—he was just a fool who had accepted a golf cart upgrade to ignore Crystal's late-night walk, only to get nervous and check on Bobby anyway. Tammy Beaumont was just neighborhood noise, trading secrets for favors but completely innocent of any real malice. 

The real enemy wasn't inside the Cove. The real enemy was institutional.

"The red herrings are burned, Tee," I said, a knowing half-smirk returning to my face. "Dale's trail-cam drifter was just a red herring. Tammy's bathtub wine was just gossip. The system wanted us to turn on each other so we wouldn't look at the signatures at the courthouse."

"Found-family stays together," Teepop said, his large hand wrapping around mine. "But the clock is still running, Brooke. We have the drive, but we still need to prove the historical link to the Monday council meeting. The council suits won't care about a missing resident memo. They care about documentation."

"Then we open the third folder," I said, pointing to the screen. "`REGISTRY_89-0412_RAW`."

## PAGE 96

[ART: Brooke pointing at a digital lab analysis spreadsheet showing high levels of the herbicide Hexazinone circled in bright red; the chemical structure is displayed in detail]

I clicked on the third directory folder, the screen instantly displaying a high-resolution scan of a thirty-year-old laboratory spreadsheet. 

It was the raw, unredacted chemical analysis of the tupelo ridge soil from nineteen-eighty-nine. The columns of data were covered in handwritten red ink, matching the physical toxicology report I’d found in the courthouse vault.

"Hexazinone," I said, my clinical eyes scanning the chemical structure. "Lethal concentrations. But look at the corporate logo at the top of the lab sheet, Tee. *'Acadian Timber and Land Consolidation.'*"

"Acadian Timber," Teepop said, his legal logic clicking into place. "Owned by Conrad Mouton’s father, Conrad Sr. They were using the freshwater herbicide to clear the tupelo hardwoods so they could legally redefine the ridge as 'uninhabitable marshland' and buy the family lots for pennies. But Clara Hebert saw them doing it. She saw them dumping the drums into the ridge water."

"And that's why they killed her," I whispered, the horror of the thirty-year-old crime finally laid bare. "She wasn't a runaway. She was a witness. They poisoned the land, and then they poisoned the girl to protect the consolidation. And McMurrary signed the runaway certificate to bury the chemical evidence so he could get his medical school tuition paid."

The Cinder Cove Phase Three consolidation wasn't just a modern land grab. 

It was the final burial of a thirty-year-old murder.

## PAGE 97

[ART: close shot of Teepop's laptop screen displaying the Delaware shell corporation's legal bank guarantee form; the routing numbers lead to 'Lafayette Aesthetic Trust']

"Look at the modern bank guarantee, Bug," Teepop said, zooming in on the financial clearances Bayou Ridge Holdings used to buy the marsh lots. 

The Delaware shell corporation's legal bank guarantee form was a clean, digital copy. The bank routing numbers were identical to the Lafayette Aesthetic Trust clearances we’d traced earlier, proving Dr. Scott McMurrary was the sole silent partner financing the entire Phase Three development.

"He's been funding Crystal's HOA clearances for three years," Teepop explained. "He paid Crystal's ex-husband Conrad to expedite the Section Twelve clearances. He financed Dale's golf cart upgrades. He’s the money, Brooke. He’s using his plastic surgery fortune to buy the silence of the parish council."

"He’s buying the ground," I said, "so he can pour the concrete over Clara's grave. If the retention pond is built, the physical soil—the exact tupelo ridge where Bobby found the hexazinone sample #1—is sealed under six inches of reinforced concrete forever. No state auditor can ever drill it. No sheriff can ever search it."

The blueprint was a perfect, silent execution. 

"The doctor," I said, "is trying to write the final file."

## PAGE 98

[ART: Brooke comparing the digital signature of Dr. Scott McMurrary on the 1989 death certificate side-by-side with his modern signature on the Cinder Cove easement guarantee]

I pulled out my notebook labeled *'Things Folks Said Too Fast'*, comparing my sketches of the courthouse signatures side-by-side with the digital scans on the screen. 

The comparison was striking. 

On the left was Dr. Scott McMurrary’s signature from the nineteen-eighty-nine death certificate—a sharp, clinical scrawl written with a young doctor's ambition. On the right was his modern signature on the Cinder Cove easement guarantee—the exact same clinical scrawl, the same long, sweeping tail on the 'S', but written with the heavy, controlled precision of a man who had spent thirty years hiding a secret behind a clean margin.

"The handwriting is identical," I said, my finger tracing the digital lines. "He hasn't changed his stroke, Tee. He still writes the 'M' with that sharp, surgical hook."

"It's the same hand, Bug," Teepop said. "The hand that certified the lie thirty years ago is the hand signing the checks today. If we present this comparison to the parish council at the Monday hearing, we can prove McMurrary has a direct, personal conflict of interest in the zoning consolidation. The state will have to freeze the project."

"But we need one more thing," I said, my eyes narrowing. "We need the second signature block on Clara’s original autopsy sheet. The intern signature that certified McMurrary’s runaway certificate."

"Let's open it," Teepop said.

## PAGE 99

[ART: close shot on the digital scan of Clara Hebert's original, unredacted autopsy report; Brooke's gloved hand is pointing at the bottom right signature block, where the name 'Kat Barnett, Intern Assistant' is clearly visible]

Teepop clicked on the final file in the directory: `clara_hebert_autopsy_original.pdf`. 

The digital page opened slow, the old typewriter text resolving under the flickering kitchen light. It was the original, unredacted autopsy report of Clara Hebert—the physical record that had been locked in the basement vaults for thirty years. 

My eyes bypassed the clinical descriptions of brackish water and lung damage, sliding straight down to the bottom right of the page, where the two official signature blocks were located.

The first was Dr. Scott McMurrary’s clinical scrawl, certifying the cause of death as accidental drowning.

The second signature block—the young nursing student intern who had certified the laboratory toxicology panel, the one who had failed to report the lethal levels of hexazinone in Clara's blood—was written in a soft, elegant hand that made my heart freeze in my chest.

The signature read: 

*Kat Barnett — Intern Assistant.*

Kat Barnett. My mother. 

The saintly, loving survivor who had slept in cars with me to escape Larry's threats. The Miss Everything of Eret who had slowly been worn down to survival-math caution by fear.

"My mama," I whispered, the room going completely silent, the flashlight slipping from my gloved fingers to clatter against the kitchen counter.

"Brooke..." Teepop said, his voice dropping into a shocked, painful register. "No."

## PAGE 100

[ART: dramatic close shot on Brooke's face; her eyes are wide with shock and devastating realization under the kitchen light; the computer screen reflects 'Kat Barnett' across her jawline, ending on a massive cliffhanger]

I stared at the screen, the cool, digital light reflecting the name *Kat Barnett* across my jawline, casting a long, skeletal shadow down my neck. 

My mother. 

The woman who had taught me how to love soft, the woman whose split lips and bruises I had watched Larry’s friends excuse as a family matter. The woman who had spent her whole life trying to keep us safe by keeping her head down and never making a report.

She hadn't just been a victim of Larry’s protection. She had been the one who signed the paper that protected the Moutons. 

She was the assistant who had certified Dr. McMurrary’s false autopsy certificate.

The Eret legacy and the Da Berry coroner case didn't just sit close to the Cove. They sat directly inside my own home, written in my mother's elegant, looping ink.

"She signed it, Tee," I whispered, the tears finally burning my eyes. "My mama signed the lie."

Cricket let out a soft, mourning whine from the passenger seat of my heart. 

The case was no longer just about saving the park. 

It was about my mother's record.

And I was going to correct it.
