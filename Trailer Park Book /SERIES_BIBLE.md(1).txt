TRAILER PARK FANTASIES — Series Bible (v2, locked)
Single source of truth. Feed this to ChatGPT, autonovel, and the build script so names, places, tone, look, and continuity stay consistent across all 150 pages. When any tool disagrees with this document, this document wins.

Author pen name: Bug Barnett


Logline
A medically trained, forensically seasoned trailer-park detective investigates a neighbor's disappearance and uncovers a land-grab conspiracy threatening her community — a Southern-Gothic mystery that's atmospheric and slow-burn, but funny, warm, and full of people who think they're living their most glamorous life and have no idea how ridiculous they are. (Trailer chic.)
Setting
Crystal's Cove Mobile Home Community — rural Acadiana, Louisiana, at golden-hour-into-plum dusk. String lights, lit trailers, a water tower, power lines, plastic flamingos, a hazy bayou treeline. Front-gate sign lost its L: "uxury Living at Affordable Prices." Established 1972. Everyone treats the Cove like a luxury resort. It is not. Satellite locations: a startup-nonprofit office, Crowflies Catholic (volleyball), parish offices, coroner records, drone test fields, the Barnett family kitchen.

PART I — THE BARNETT CORE (protagonist + investigation team)
===============================================================================
Brooke "Bug" Barnett — PROTAGONIST, master detective
Late 30s. Former nurse. Former coroner-case assistant. Wife. Mother of three. Current unofficial truth-finder of Crystal's Cove. She is the boss and the final decision-maker, always.

Public face (the glamour is armor, not cosplay): trailer-chic Sherlock — denim romper, tan tooled cowboy boots, long detective duster, deerstalker-with-Western-edge hat, chunky turquoise necklace, turquoise cuffs and rings, bee tattoo on one forearm (→ "Bug"), fine-line script on the opposite wrist, brass magnifying glass, "Case Files — Crystal's Cove" book, long dark wavy hair, warm brown eyes, knowing half-smirk. She dresses bold because she refuses to be the woman who does the invisible work and gets ignored.

True skillset: classically trained nurse; investigative education came from working with Dr. Scott McMurrary (doctor-turned-plastic-surgeon and parish coroner in Da Berry). She cataloged, documented, examined, organized, and talked to investigators. Her superpower: she notices what changed — the missing receipt, the too-clean wound, the moved pill bottle, the truck parked crooked on purpose.

Personality: reads people like road signs. Doesn't rush, doesn't flinch, never mistakes loud for confident. Steel spine, warm heart. Tender with a scared kid, ruthless with a liar, funny when the room's too tense to breathe. Not cold — controlled.

Emotional wound: she's always the one who holds the room together. Her deepest fear isn't danger — it's missing something important when someone trusted her to see it. Every case is personal.

Detective philosophy: she doesn't chase clues, she follows consequences.

Motto: "People lie. Bodies don't. Paperwork tries, but it's bad at it."

Signature behaviors: photos before touching anything; gloves in the duster pocket; a notebook labeled "Things Folks Said Too Fast"; notices medical inconsistencies in casual talk; smells for cleaning products before entering a room; reads hands, shoes, posture, meds, silence; humor to disarm, silence to break.

Reputation: known around the parish as The Bayou Bloodhound — she sniffs out danger, lies, and root cause before anyone else sees the pattern. ("Don't lie to Brooke Barnett. That woman's the Bayou Bloodhound — she'll smell the truth before you finish lyin'.")

Origin / core wound: Brooke grew up in the one-horse town of Eret with a loving, saintly mother, Kat (once the town's "Miss Everything" — cheerleader, beautiful, admired — slowly worn down to survival-math caution by fear), and a dangerous stepfather, Larry, a raging alcoholic who worked for the Eret PD. When Kat called the police, Larry's coworkers showed up and let him "sleep it off"; Brooke and Kat slept in cars and borrowed rooms. That was the first case Brooke ever lived inside — no file, no report, no justice — and it taught her that truth nobody writes down gets treated like gossip. Her hyper-awareness isn't fragility or magic; it's survival intelligence (reading exits, moods, fake smiles, rehearsed stories, the rot under charm) that nursing, coroner work, Rena, and Teepop later refined into method. Larry eventually had a stroke, Kat divorced him, and he spent ~20 years in a nursing home — finally given the care, documentation, and people-obligated-to-notice he never gave anyone. Handle with moral complexity, never cartoon revenge. Brooke's trauma deepens her; it does not define her. She is evidence-led, not reckless.

Why she investigates: she recognizes the pattern — "crazy," "dramatic," "drunk," "difficult," "attention-seeking" are words people use when they want to stop listening. Phrases like "he didn't mean it / he was drinking / we handled it internally / no need to make a report" light her up — quietly, dangerously. Bobby is exactly the kind of person a community explains away; his tabbed, dated, cross-referenced complaint binder isn't noise — it's "a man trying to be believed." Everyone else asks where did Bobby go? Brooke asks who benefits from everyone thinking Bobby was the problem?

Character spine: WANT — find out what happened to Bobby. NEED — accept she can fight for the truth without carrying every old wound alone. FEAR — missing a warning sign. LIE SHE BELIEVES — "If I don't see it first, someone gets hurt." TRUTH SHE LEARNS — she's not alone anymore (Rena, Teepop, the girls, the messy Cove family). MORAL CODE — documentation is protection; truth belongs to the powerless first; nobody gets dismissed just for being difficult.

Character tagline: "She doesn't solve mysteries. She corrects the record."
Henry "Teepop" Barnett — husband, support system (never her handler)
Healthcare-law attorney turned stay-at-home dad and startup-nonprofit ecosystem operator. Built like a brick house, dresses like athletic-nerd-meets-Southern-party-intellectual: patterned button-downs, caps, sharp sunglasses, a fringed jacket when he's feeling himself, polished suits when needed.

Function: through his nonprofit work with ~50 founders he gives Brooke access to drones (air/land/water), engineers, hardware hackers, database people, legal logic, and healthcare- compliance reasoning. He brings options; Brooke makes the call. He is the bridge between old-school investigation and new-school tools.

Marriage dynamic (an emotional anchor of the series): no dumb-husband trope, no nagging-wife trope, no manufactured marital drama. Adult, warm, funny, lived-in. He worries about her but never controls her; she trusts him but never depends on him to think for her.

"You want legal, technical, or wildly irresponsible?" — "All three. In that order."

Recurring scenes: late-night kitchen-counter strategy after the kids are down; mapping case facts on a whiteboard while holding a lunchbox or volleyball bag; bringing Brooke a founder's "mostly waterproof" drone; finding the loophole in a parish filing; saying something deeply romantic in a completely practical way ("I packed your gloves, charged your flashlight, and highlighted the statute they're probably violating. Also, you look dangerous. I love you."). He knows Brooke carries old danger in her nervous system and never treats her as broken — he treats her as brilliant ("You don't have to scan every exit when I'm standing next to you. But I know you will, so I already checked the back door.").
Miss Rena Hebert — mentor, forensic elder, moral anchor
Classy, fierce Southern Black woman, mid-60s. Iconic well-pressed green overalls. Quiet authority — people lower their voices when she enters. Volleyball coach at Crowflies Catholic; godmother to one of Brooke's daughters (so she's family, not just a colleague).

Backstory & function: has seen more coroner/forensic cases than most investigators ever will. Became Brooke's mentor in the coroner years; taught her the difference between curiosity ("what happened?") and discipline ("what can I prove?"). Reads a scene photo and catches what everyone missed; flags bad documentation; separates instinct from proof; delivers the series' deepest moral lines.

Voice: short, elegant, cutting. "That ain't a clue. That's decoration." / "People clean what they're ashamed of." / "Don't ask what they said — ask what they needed you to believe." / "Baby, instinct is a bell. Evidence is the door you open after you hear it."

Rena also understands Brooke's Eret history but won't let her drown in it — she's the one who can tell Brooke when she's chasing proof versus chasing an old ghost ("That little girl in Eret learned how to survive. But you, Brooke? You learned how to investigate. Don't confuse the two.").

Next step is converting that line into an actual season plot thread of her own.)
The Three Daughters — emotional stakes, not plot props
They show what Brooke protects: the idea that families deserve safety, truth, and a fighting chance. Usage rule: not danger-bait. Mostly home-life texture, humor, time pressure, moral clarity. They reveal emotional truths, not hard evidence. Locked roster:

	•	Bernie Barnett (oldest) — quiet observer; sharp-eyed, dry-witted; catches the detail nobody else caught and remembers who said what and when. "Bernie doesn't chase attention. She catches patterns." ("I'm not nosy. Y'all are just obvious.")
	•	Sonia Barnett (middle) — volleyball heart; athletic, steady, reads the emotional truth before the adults say it; closest to Rena's kind of strength. Plays for the Crowflies Catholic Crusaders (Rena's team), #8. "She brings calm to the noise." ("You can tell when grown-ups are scared. They start talking around the truth.")
	•	Alix Kat Barnett (youngest) — "chaos with a compass"; notes everything, forgets nothing; the one who overhears and blurts; future solver of all problems. Attends Crystal's Cove Middle School (Seahawks), #13. Her middle name Kat honors Brooke's mother (lineage thread → Ch.14). ("I'm not trying to be nosy. I'm just trying to help.")
Kat (mother) & Larry (former stepfather) — backstory figures
Kat — Brooke's loving, saintly, surviving mother; once the town's "Miss Everything," worn down by fear but never weak. The source of Brooke's compassion and her fury. Appears in memory, phone calls, and quiet beats; always part of Brooke's inner weather. ("Baby, you always did see what everybody else pretended not to." — "Somebody had to.") Larry — the dangerous, institutionally-protected alcoholic stepfather from Eret PD. Not the Season One villain, but the emotional origin of Brooke's intolerance for protected men. Echoes whenever someone excuses a powerful man. Moral complexity, never cartoon.
Dr. Scott McMurrary — institutional gateway (NOT the emotional mentor)
Former boss; primary-care doctor turned plastic surgeon; parish coroner in Da Berry. Smart, controlled, ambitious, polished, a little too comfortable in gray areas — not necessarily a villain, but the reader should always wonder if he knows more than he says. Brooke respects what she learned under him but doesn't fully trust him. Re-enters when Brooke needs an old report, toxicology, a parish death record, or a medical link to Bobby's case.

"You always were thorough." — "No. I was trained by women who had to be."

===============================================================================

	•	Crystal Mouton — park manager / self-crowned HOA queen. Runs the Cove like a lifestyle brand: velvet scrunchie, bedazzled glasses on a chain, monogrammed clipboard, managerial athleisure. Motto "Standards don't lower themselves." Will protect her empire at any cost.

	•	Sheila — Ricky's autonomous lawnmower (Roomba-taped-to-a-Husqvarna). Blade assembly, grabber arm, an "aggression module." Keeps wandering into crime scenes by accident — equal parts innovation, chaos, and zero situational awareness.
	•	Joy Lee "Sugar" McCoy — loud, big-hearted neighbor; part-time church cook, full-time neighborhood news network. First to sound the alarm about Bobby's disappearance. Brings banana pudding to every crisis. (Renamed from the draft's "Joy Lee 'Cricket' McCoy.")
	•	Bobby Boudreaux — the missing resident. 57, Lot 5. Owed money to half the park, filed a stack of parish complaints (potholes to palm trees), truck always parked crooked across two spots, prized garden-gnome collection. Loud shirt, loud opinions. Last seen the night of the crawfish boil. (Always Bobby Boudreaux — never "Doc Bourbon." It's a crawfish boil, not lobster — this is Acadiana.)
Cricket — Bug's terrier sidekick
Scruffy gray terrier. Loyal, alert. Growls at liars, wags at truth-tellers, right more often than the sheriff. The dog is the ONLY Cricket — no human shares the name. (If Joy Lee keeps a pet on her card, it's clearly her own separate dog, not Cricket.)

PART III — THE INVESTIGATION ENGINE (recurring structure)
===============================================================================

	•	Brooke sees the human truth (instinct, medical knowledge, forensic discipline).
	•	Rena checks the forensic truth (old-school wisdom, moral clarity, case memory).
	•	Teepop checks the legal/technical truth (records, contracts, startup tools, drones).
	•	Ricky accidentally introduces a tool. Sheila accidentally discovers something.
	•	Everyone else creates noise. Brooke turns noise into evidence.

Tech rule: tools assist, never replace. Keep them funny, local, and messy — drone aerial mapping, after-dark thermal scans, a rover that gets stuck under Bobby's trailer, a bayou water drone, porch-cam audio cleanup, a "smart mailbox," Sheila's accidental GPS logs, a founder's AI transcription tool that mishears Cajun names hilariously, a 3D scan proving one gnome is missing. Brooke still cracks it because she understands people.

PART IV — SEASON ONE THROUGHLINE
=============================================================================== Main mystery: Bobby Boudreaux vanishes from Lot 5 — truck still there, TV still on, one garden gnome missing. Everyone assumes he skipped town over money. Brooke doesn't buy it: Bobby was petty and broke, but he was also scared.

Hidden truth: Bobby's complaint binder shows he was documenting something far bigger than potholes — tied to illegal land acquisition / environmental contamination / parish corruption, and possibly a suspicious death from years ago that connects to old coroner records (Brooke's past with McMurrary, and a case Rena never forgot).

Midseason reveal: Bobby didn't vanish because he owed money. He vanished because one of his complaints was right.

Late-season reveal: the missing garden gnome wasn't quirky — it was a marker. Inside: a flash drive / soil sample / key / rolled complaint copy / old coroner-case photo.

Finale setup: Bobby's disappearance connects to an old case Rena never let go of.

Rena: "I knew this would come back. I just prayed it wouldn't come through you."

Opening beat (Ch.1): Brooke arrives after Sugar sounds the alarm. Dale's already worsening it with online-bought perimeter tape; Sheila has rolled through the scene's edge; Crystal's managing optics; Tammy's pretending not to know everything. Brooke doesn't yell — she puts on gloves. That's when it stops being neighborhood drama and becomes a case.

"That's not a mess. That's a message." / "Who saw the truck before the rain?"

Ch.1 cold-open narration (Brooke VO): "I learned early that a house can lie. A clean kitchen can lie. A smiling man can lie. A police report can lie by never getting written. My mama taught me how to love soft. Larry taught me how danger enters a room. Nursing taught me what pain looks like when people try to hide it. Rena taught me the truth deserves a clean record. So when folks tell me a missing man was probably just trouble taking itself elsewhere, I don't nod along. I look closer. Because trouble doesn't always leave. Sometimes it gets moved. Sometimes it gets cleaned up. Sometimes it gets filed under 'nothing to see here.' And I have made a life out of seeing anyway."

Key scene — the binder: the room laughs Bobby's complaint binder off ("Bobby complained if the sun came up crooked"). Brooke turns a tabbed, dated, cross-referenced page and goes still: "No. This isn't noise." Dale: "Then what is it?" Brooke, to the room: "A man trying to be believed." It lands because it's not just about Bobby — it's about Kat, and every person filed under "difficult."

PART V — TONE, THEMES, VISUAL STYLE
=============================================================================== Tone: Southern-Gothic atmosphere + camp humor + genuine emotional weight, in balance. Misdirection and red herrings, slow-burn (the Brooke/Teepop marriage deepening under pressure, not "will they/won't they"), found-family warmth, non-linear trauma recovery. Comedy engine: everyone believes they're fabulous; no one realizes they're absurd.

Themes: found family; healing (Rena taught Brooke not to numb herself to pain; Teepop reminds her she doesn't carry it alone); community secrets (every lot has one — funny, tragic, or dangerous); class & power (working-class people have to document everything just to be believed — that's Brooke's lane; she believes the people nobody listens to).

Chloe Walsh is a directional thematic reference only (found family, healing, slow burn, small-town dynamics). All prose is original — never imitate or reproduce her text.

Visual style: bold cel-shaded illustration in the spirit of a GTA loading screen — thick outlines, painterly gradients, semi-realistic faces, high-contrast lighting. Trailer-chic palette: dusty rose, golden-hour peach, turquoise pops, denim blue, deep plum shadows. Reference the cover/key art and the character sheets for design, palette, and atmosphere.
