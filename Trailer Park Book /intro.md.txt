Premise
A medically trained, forensically seasoned trailer-park detective investigates a neighbor's disappearance and uncovers a land-grab conspiracy threatening her community — Southern-Gothic and slow-burn, but funny, warm, and full of people who think they're living their most glamorous life and have no idea how ridiculous they are. Trailer chic.
Setting
Crystal's Cove Mobile Home Community, rural Acadiana, Louisiana, est. 1972. Golden-hour-into- plum dusk, string lights, a water tower, plastic flamingos, a hazy bayou treeline. The front sign lost its L: "uxury Living at Affordable Prices." Everyone treats the Cove like a luxury resort. It is not. Satellite locations: a startup-nonprofit office, Crowflies Catholic (volleyball), parish offices, coroner records (Da Berry), drone test fields, the Barnett kitchen.

Tone
Atmospheric Southern-Gothic + camp humor + genuine emotional weight, held in balance. Comedy engine: everyone believes they're fabulous; no one realizes they're absurd. The slow burn is the Brooke/Teepop marriage deepening under pressure, not "will they/won't they." Found-family warmth. Original prose — Chloe Walsh is a directional theme reference only; never imitate her.
Investigation engine (recurring structure)
Brooke sees the human truth → Rena checks the forensic truth → Teepop checks the legal/technical truth → Ricky accidentally introduces a tool / Sheila accidentally discovers something → everyone else makes noise → Brooke turns noise into evidence. Tech is funny, local, and messy; it assists, it never replaces her read on people.
