TRAILER PARK FANTASIES — Season One Beat Sheet (15 chapters / 150 pages)
Read intro.md and SERIES_BIBLE.md first. Each chapter ≈ 10 pages (~2,000–2,500 words). Hit the listed clues, honor the misdirection, land the character turn, and end on the cliffhanger. Plant clues fairly (the reader could, in theory, solve it). Keep the tone: camp + heart + atmosphere.


⚠ OPEN DECISION — lock before Chapter 10 (affects Ch. 10, 12, 15)
Is Bobby alive or dead?

	•	DEFAULT (this beat sheet): Bobby is ALIVE. He went to ground when he realized one complaint was right and made him a target. The conspiracy has been hunting his evidence cache; the gnome is the marker that leads to it. The story's actual death is the old coroner-case victim the land grab is built on — that's the record Brooke ultimately corrects.
	•	ALTERNATE: Bobby is DEAD (aligns with the game prototype; Crystal as proximate killer). If chosen: Ch.10 recovers evidence of the killing rather than a flip; Ch.12 recovers his body; Ch.15 resolves to proving the killer. The land-grab/old-case spine is unchanged. Everything else below holds under either option.


Ch. 1 — Welcome to Crystal's Cove (pp. 1–10)
Purpose: establish world, cast, voice, and the inciting disappearance. Beats: open on Brooke's cold-open VO (see bible, Ch.1 narration). The morning after the crawfish boil, Sugar pounds on doors — Bobby's gone, truck still there, TV still on. Brooke arrives to chaos: Dale stringing online-bought "perimeter" tape, Sheila idling at the scene's edge, Crystal managing optics, Tammy pretending not to know everything. Brooke doesn't yell — she snaps on gloves. Quick-tour the Cove and its absurd glamour through her eyes. Clues planted: crooked truck across two spots; one garden gnome missing from a tidy row; the complaint binder exists; a faint bleach smell at the trailer door. Misdirection: everyone's first theory — "Bobby owed half the park; he finally ran." Character turn: the gloves change the air; this stops being gossip and becomes a case. Cliffhanger: Brooke flips the binder open, goes still — "This isn't noise. It's a man trying to be believed."
Ch. 2 — False Leads (pp. 11–20)
Purpose: burn down the obvious theory; introduce the investigation engine. Beats: Brooke works the debt angle and it won't hold — the timeline's wrong (he was "leaving" but left the things a runner takes). Teepop pulls filings; Rena reads scene photos and flags sloppy assumptions. Comedy: a founder's AI transcription tool mangles Cajun names. Clues: a receipt/timestamp that contradicts "he left willingly"; the binder is missing a tab — a page was removed. Misdirection: Dale's trail-cam "evidence" points at a drifter (red herring). Character turn: Teepop sets the marriage tone — backs her play, checks the back door. Cliffhanger: the removed page's tab heading is still legible: a parish lot-line filing.
Ch. 3 — Trailer Tricks (pp. 21–30)
Purpose: read the scene like a coroner; stage-vs-real. Beats: close search of Lot 5. Brooke reads what was moved and cleaned (nurse/coroner eye). Sheila drags something out from under the trailer. Tammy trades a secret for a favor. Clues: the trailer was staged to read as a voluntary exit; an object Sheila surfaces ties to the land filing. Misdirection: suspicion swings toward Tammy (knows too much) or Dale (too eager). Character turn: Crystal leans on Brooke to drop it — "that binder's just Bobby being Bobby." Cliffhanger: the removed page referenced a specific lot line — and it isn't Bobby's lot.
Ch. 4 — Bayou Secrets (pp. 31–40)
Purpose: push the complaint thread out onto the land. Beats: follow it into the bayou/back lots. Teepop's drone (aerial + water) maps disturbed ground and a fresh survey stake. Someone's quietly consolidating lots. Rena spots something that visibly unsettles her — first seed of her thread. Clues: survey markers; a shell entity buying parcels. Misdirection: looks like a generic developer; the human target is still hidden. Character turn: Brooke clocks Rena's reaction and files it. Cliffhanger: one of Bobby's complaints named a name.
Ch. 5 — Confrontation (pp. 41–50)
Purpose: first direct accusation; raise the danger. Beats: Brooke confronts a stonewaller (parish official and/or Crystal) and hits institutional language — "we handled it internally," "no need to make a report." It lights her up (Larry echo). Clues: confirmation that one complaint was quietly buried/acted on. Misdirection: the stonewaller seems like the villain — too clean to be the whole story. Character turn: Brooke names the pattern to herself — this is what they did to Kat. Cliffhanger: an anonymous warning lands — drop it. The case is now dangerous.
Ch. 6 — The Blueprint (pp. 51–60)  ◆ MIDSEASON REVEAL
Purpose: reveal the shape of the scheme; flip the premise. Beats: the land-grab blueprint comes into focus — consolidation tied to contamination or a development play. Teepop finds the legal mechanism/loophole. Clues: the plat/blueprint itself. Reveal: Bobby didn't vanish over money — one of his complaints was RIGHT. Character turn: Brooke recommits — not curiosity now, conviction. Cliffhanger: the scheme rests on an old parish death — a coroner case.
Ch. 7 — Lot Lines (pp. 61–70)
Purpose: map the buying center; reintroduce McMurrary; deepen the old case. Beats: who benefits from consolidating the lots? Brooke needs an old coroner report — back to McMurrary in Da Berry. Tension: "You always did have a problem leaving sealed files sealed." Clues: an old death ruled "accidental" that wasn't. Misdirection: McMurrary looks like the architect (he's a gateway, not the top — keep him gray). Character turn: Brooke weighs respect vs. distrust for her old boss. Cliffhanger: the old case quietly ties back to Rena.
Ch. 8 — Inside Job (pp. 71–80)
Purpose: the betrayal inside the Cove. Beats: someone inside has been feeding the scheme. Crystal's optics-management gets a motive — she's protecting her empire and a cut of the deal. Her clipboard/records contradict her story. Clues: Crystal's paperwork places her where she said she wasn't. Misdirection: a sympathetic ally looks compromised (resolve fairly later). Character turn: found-family fracture — trust inside the Cove cracks. Cliffhanger: Brooke works out where the missing gnome's contents point.
Ch. 9 — High Stakes (pp. 81–90)
Purpose: raise personal + communal stakes; start the clock. Beats: intimidation escalates (pressure on the family — not danger-bait for the girls; think a threat, a towed truck, a frozen account). If the deal closes at the parish vote, the Cove is gone. Time pressure set. Clues: the gnome marker is located (a where, not yet a what). Character turn: Teepop and Brooke choose the fight together, eyes open. Cliffhanger: they recover the gnome's hidden contents.
Ch. 10 — Double-Cross (pp. 91–100)
Purpose: turn the screw; convert evidence into a named adversary. Beats (DEFAULT): the gnome's contents (drive/sample/photo) expose a double-cross — a player turns on the conspiracy, or a trusted face is implicated. Bobby's survival/whereabouts comes into focus (he hid himself; the cache was insurance). Clues: hard evidence linking the land grab to the old coroner case and a present player. Misdirection resolved: the earlier red herrings (drifter / Tammy / Dale) are cleared. Character turn: Brooke realizes how close to home this old case sits. Cliffhanger: the evidence implicates someone the reader didn't want it to. (ALTERNATE: the contents prove Bobby was killed to bury the right complaint.)
Ch. 11 — The Trial (pp. 101–110)
Purpose: the first reckoning — and the system's resistance. Beats: not necessarily a courtroom — a parish hearing / HOA showdown / DA sit-down where Brooke corrects the record with documentation. The system resists (Larry echo: protected people). Clues: the documented case becomes undeniable on its face. Character turn: Brooke holds the line publicly; Rena steadies her. Cliffhanger: a procedural twist threatens to bury it all again.
Ch. 12 — Night of the Fireflies (pp. 111–120)
Purpose: atmospheric set-piece; resolve Bobby's fate. Beats (DEFAULT): a night operation — drone search / stakeout / the adversary's move. Brooke makes contact with Bobby (alive, hidden) or finds proof of where he's been. A quiet, real Brooke/Teepop beat under the string lights. Clues: physical confirmation of what happened the night of the boil. Character turn: the difficult man everyone wrote off is, in fact, a witness who was right. Cliffhanger: the adversary makes a desperate, escalating move. (ALTERNATE: the night search recovers Bobby's body — grief reframes the stakes.)
Ch. 13 — Flashbacks & Revelations (pp. 121–130)
Purpose: braid the backstories; pay off the old case. Beats: Eret (Brooke/Kat/Larry), Rena's old case, and McMurrary's role converge. The original coroner case is laid bare — what was softened, by whom, and why. Clues: the full truth of the old death. Character turn: Rena — "I knew this would come back. I just prayed it wouldn't come through you." Brooke's wound and her method finally fuse into one thing. Cliffhanger: one last piece is needed to make it provable, not just true.
Ch. 14 — Treasure Hunt & Lineage (pp. 131–140)
Purpose: final evidence hunt; found family rallies. Beats: "lineage" = inheritance/land/family ties; the "treasure" is the proof that locks the chain — possibly Bobby's full documentation cache. The Cove shows up for each other (camp + heart peak). Ricky/Sheila get one last accidental save. Clues: complete chain of custody — the record is now airtight. Character turn: Brooke accepts she isn't carrying it alone. Cliffhanger: the final confrontation is set.
Ch. 15 — Coup & Resolution (pp. 141–150)
Purpose: the takedown, the corrected record, the landing. Beats: the community's "coup" against the land grab; the powerful are held to account with moral complexity, not easy justice or revenge punchlines. Bobby's fate fully resolved. The record is corrected on the books. Resolution: the Cove is saved (for now); marriage / family / found-family beats; Rena's thread closed with dignity. Brooke's arc completes — she fought for the truth without carrying every old wound alone. Season-two hook: a new thread opens (a name on the consolidated plat that doesn't fit; a second gnome; a call from Eret) — small, not a cliff drop.
